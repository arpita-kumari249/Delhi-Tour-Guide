Fifteenth-century tombs resting among lawns, trees and morning walkers.

Insights

Lodhi Garden is a large public park of roughly 90 acres, and one of the most popular green spaces in Delhi. It surrounds monuments from the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties of the 15th and early 16th centuries.

The main monuments include the tomb of Muhammad Shah, the tomb of Sikandar Lodi, the Bara Gumbad with its mosque, and the Sheesh Gumbad, which once had glazed blue tiles. The Athpula, a Mughal-era bridge, crosses a small stream in the park.

The park was landscaped in the 1930s and called Lady Willingdon Park before it was renamed after independence. Today it is loved by joggers, photographers, families and picnickers, and it also has a bonsai park and a glasshouse.

Location

Lodhi Garden is located on Lodhi Road, near Khanna Market, New Delhi – 110003.

BY METRO

The nearest station is Jor Bagh on the Yellow Line, with Khan Market (Violet Line) also within reach.

NEARBY PLACES

Safdarjung's Tomb, Khan Market, India Habitat Centre, India International Centre and Humayun's Tomb are nearby.

Useful Information

The garden is free to enter and open daily from early morning to evening, so check the current hours at the gate. It is quietest on weekday mornings and busiest on weekend evenings. Winter, from October to March, is the best season for walking here.
