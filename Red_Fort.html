Insights

The Red Fort (Lal Qila) was built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan as the palace-fort of his new capital, Shahjahanabad. Construction took place between 1639 and 1648, and the fort takes its name from its massive red sandstone walls. It was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.

Visitors enter through the Lahori Gate and walk along the covered Chhatta Chowk bazaar to the Naubat Khana (drum house). Inside are the Diwan-i-Aam (Hall of Public Audience), the Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience), the Rang Mahal and the Moti Masjid.

The fort has been central to Indian history. Its last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was tried here after the Revolt of 1857. Since 1947 the Prime Minister of India has hoisted the national flag from its ramparts every Independence Day.

Museums within the complex cover Indian history and the freedom struggle. In the evening, a sound and light show tells the story of the fort.

Location

The Red Fort is located on Netaji Subhash Marg, Chandni Chowk, Old Delhi – 110006, facing the busy Chandni Chowk market road.

BY METRO

The nearest station is Lal Qila on the Violet Line. Chandni Chowk station on the Yellow Line is also within walking or rickshaw distance.

NEARBY PLACES

Jama Masjid, Chandni Chowk, Digambar Jain Temple, Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib and Fatehpuri Masjid are all close by, so the fort combines well with a day in Old Delhi.

Useful Information

The Red Fort is closed on Mondays. Buy tickets from official counters or the official booking platform, and expect security checks at the entrance. Large bags are usually restricted. Allow two to three hours, and check timings and prices on the official ASI website before you go.
