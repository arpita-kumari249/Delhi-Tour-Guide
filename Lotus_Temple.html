Insights

The Lotus Temple is a Bahá'í House of Worship, completed in 1986 and designed by the architect Fariborz Sahba. Its shape resembles a half-opened lotus flower, with 27 free-standing marble petals arranged in clusters of three to form nine sides.

The building is surrounded by nine reflecting pools and green gardens. The central hall is a calm, open space with no idols, altar or sermon. People of all religions are welcome to sit in silence and pray or meditate.

The Lotus Temple has won architectural awards and is one of the most visited buildings in the world. It is especially striking at dusk, when the marble glows against the sky.

Location

The Lotus Temple is located at Lotus Temple Road, Bahapur, Shambhu Dayal Bagh, Kalkaji, New Delhi – 110019.

BY METRO

The nearest station is Kalkaji Mandir on the Violet Line. From there it is a short walk or rickshaw ride to the temple.

NEARBY PLACES

ISKCON Temple, Kalkaji Mandir, Nehru Place and Okhla Bird Sanctuary are in the surrounding area.

Useful Information

The Lotus Temple is closed on Mondays and entry is free. Visitors are expected to remain silent inside the prayer hall, and photography inside is not allowed. Dress modestly, and expect a queue on weekends and holidays.
