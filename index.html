HISTORY

Delhi is best understood as a city built in layers, with monuments from different periods standing close to one another.
Ancient Delhi: Tradition connects the area with Indraprastha, the city mentioned in the Mahabharata. The exact historical identification is debated, but the site is associated with Purana Qila.
Medieval Delhi: From the 12th century onward, Delhi became a major centre of several Islamic dynasties, beginning with the Delhi Sultanate. This period left landmarks such as Qutub Minar, Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, Tughlaqabad Fort, and Lodhi Gardens.
Mughal Delhi: Delhi became especially important under the Mughals. Shah Jahan established Shahjahanabad in the 17th century, centred around the Red Fort and Jama Masjid. The old city around Chandni Chowk still reflects this heritage.
1857 and British rule: Delhi was a major centre of the Revolt of 1857. After the uprising, British control was strengthened, and in 1911 the British announced the transfer of their capital from Calcutta to Delhi.
New Delhi: Designed by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, New Delhi was inaugurated as the imperial capital in 1931. Its grand avenues and buildings include India Gate, Rashtrapati Bhavan, and Parliament House.
Independent India: After independence in 1947, Delhi became the capital of the Republic of India. The city expanded dramatically and became a major political, cultural, and commercial centre.
