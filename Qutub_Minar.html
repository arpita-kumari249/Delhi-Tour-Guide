Welcome to Qutub Minar

A towering victory minaret from the dawn of the Delhi Sultanate.

OPENDaily
TIMINGSunrise – Sunset

NEAREST METROQutub Minar

Insights

Qutub Minar is a five-storey tower about 72.5 metres tall, and one of the tallest brick minarets in the world. Construction began around 1193 under Qutb-ud-din Aibak, the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate, and was continued by Iltutmish. Later repairs and the upper storeys were added by Firoz Shah Tughlaq.

The tower is covered in deep carvings and bands of Quranic inscriptions in red sandstone and marble. It was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

The surrounding complex includes the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, the Alai Darwaza, the unfinished Alai Minar and the Tomb of Iltutmish. The famous Iron Pillar, over 1,500 years old, stands in the mosque courtyard and is known for resisting rust.

Location

Qutub Minar is located in Mehrauli, Seth Sarai, New Delhi – 110030, in South Delhi.

BY METRO

The nearest station is Qutub Minar on the Yellow Line. The complex is a few kilometres from the station, so take an auto-rickshaw or cab for the last stretch.

NEARBY PLACES

Mehrauli Archaeological Park, Jamali Kamali Mosque and Tomb, Hauz Khas Village and Garden of Five Senses are close by.

Useful Information

The complex is open daily. Visiting early in the morning avoids both the heat and the crowds. Climbing the minar is not currently permitted, so enjoy it from the lawns and the surrounding ruins. Check current ticket prices and timings on the official ASI website.
