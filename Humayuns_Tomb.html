Insights

Humayun's Tomb is the resting place of the second Mughal emperor, Humayun. It was commissioned by his wife, Bega Begum, and built between about 1565 and 1572. The Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas designed it.

It is regarded as the first garden-tomb in the Indian subcontinent and an important forerunner of the Taj Mahal. The tomb stands on a high platform in a Persian-style charbagh (four-part) garden, and combines red sandstone with white marble. It was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

The grounds hold other monuments, including the tomb of Isa Khan, the Barber's Tomb and the blue-domed Nila Gumbad. A major conservation project has restored the tomb and its gardens in recent years.

Location

Humayun's Tomb is located on Mathura Road, near Nizamuddin East, New Delhi – 110013.

BY METRO

The nearest station is JLN Stadium on the Violet Line. Jangpura (Violet Line) is another option, and Nizamuddin Railway Station is close by.

NEARBY PLACES

Sunder Nursery, Nizamuddin Dargah, Purana Qila and the Crafts Museum are all nearby, so they combine easily into one day.

Useful Information

The tomb is open daily during daylight hours. Late afternoon gives the best light for photography. Allow around two hours, or longer if you also visit Sunder Nursery next door. Check current ticket prices on the official ASI website.
