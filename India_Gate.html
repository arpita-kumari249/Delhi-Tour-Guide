Insights

India Gate is a 42-metre-tall war memorial standing at the eastern end of Kartavya Path (formerly Rajpath). It honours the soldiers of the British Indian Army who lost their lives in the First World War and the Third Anglo-Afghan War. The names of thousands of soldiers are inscribed on its walls.

The monument was designed by Edwin Lutyens, the architect of New Delhi. Its foundation stone was laid in 1921 and it was inaugurated in 1931. It is built mainly of red and pale sandstone on a low granite base.

Close to the gate stands the National War Memorial, opened in 2019 to honour soldiers who died in India's wars after independence. The canopy behind India Gate now holds a statue of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose.

The lawns around India Gate are among the most popular evening spots in Delhi. Families gather here for walks, ice cream and photographs, and the monument is lit up after dark.

Location

India Gate is located at Kartavya Path, Central Secretariat area, New Delhi – 110001. It lies in the centre of Lutyens' Delhi, with Rashtrapati Bhavan at the other end of the avenue.

BY METRO

The nearest stations are Central Secretariat (Yellow and Violet Lines) and Khan Market (Violet Line). Both are a few kilometres away, so most visitors continue by auto-rickshaw, cab or on foot.

FROM CONNAUGHT PLACE

Take a cab or auto-rickshaw, or take the Yellow Line from Rajiv Chowk to Central Secretariat and continue from there.

NEARBY PLACES

National War Memorial, Rashtrapati Bhavan, National Gallery of Modern Art, Purana Qila, Delhi Zoo and Khan Market are all close by.

Useful Information

India Gate is an open public space and entry is free. Evenings are the most pleasant and photogenic time to visit, but they are also the most crowded. Security checks and traffic restrictions can apply during national events such as Republic Day, so check before you go.
